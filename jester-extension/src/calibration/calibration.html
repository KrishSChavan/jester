Calibrate the eye pointer

Thirteen dots, three rounds — the same dots in the same order each time. Look at each dot, then show the camera your open palm and close it into a fist — the same open-then-closed hand that clicks. About two minutes, with a short breather between rounds.

  • Keep your head still and move only your eyes. Keep looking at the dot until your hand has closed — don't glance down at it.
  • Raise your hand out beside your shoulder, not in front of your face — the camera has to see your eyes the whole time.
  • Open fully first, then close. Open arms it, the fist takes the dot.
  • Sit the way you use your computer, about arm's length from the camera, in your usual light. You're teaching Jester how your eyes and head look from there. From across the room the pointer aims with your head instead, and that needs no calibration.
  • A dot that didn't read cleanly is offered again with the reason — twice in a round, then the round moves on and it comes round again. You can hand the last dot back yourself with Backspace.
  • Esc stops the whole calibration. Nothing is saved until the end, and your current calibration stays.

Eye tracking runs entirely on this computer, from the positions of your irises, lids and head that MediaPipe's face tracker measures. It keeps no pictures of you — only the finished calibration's numbers.
